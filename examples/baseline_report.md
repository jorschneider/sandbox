## The vacuum holds (45%)

**Trigger class:** No succession within horizon — Xi remains in office and in command through the analytic horizon. No successor is meaningfully empowered. The baseline scenario every coherent set needs.

Xi remains General Secretary, President, and CMC Chairman through 2030. The 21st Party Congress (2027) retains the loyalist core, promotes no sixth-generation figure into a credible heir track, and further dilutes age norms. Succession risk is not resolved; it compounds, with policy brittleness and elite hedging slowly increasing.

**Pathway:**

1. 21st Congress (fall 2027): Xi takes a fourth term as General Secretary; PSC reshuffle rewards loyalty over cohort renewal.
2. Propaganda intensity around Xi Jinping Thought is sustained or increased; no successor-grade elevation occurs.
3. Periodic purge waves continue as the maintenance mechanism of elite discipline.
4. By decade's end Xi is 77; the system has still produced no rule for replacing him.

**Key actors:**

- **Xi Jinping** (CCP General Secretary (3rd term, since 2022); PRC President (3rd term, since 2023; term limits abolished 2018); Central Military Commission Chairman)
- **Cai Qi** (First Secretary of the Secretariat; Director, CCP General Office; PSC rank 5)
- **Li Xi** (CCDI Secretary; PSC rank 7)
- **Zhang Youxia** (CMC Vice Chairman (senior))

**Indicators to watch (strongest first):**

- (w2) 21st Congress (expected 2027) personnel: who retires at the age line, who is retained, and whether any sixth-generation figure enters the PSC
- (w2) Sustained reduction in 'Xi Jinping Thought' / 'core' formula density in People's Daily front pages and CCTV Xinwen Lianbo
- (w1) Legacy-codifying moves: a third 'historical resolution'-type document, museum/anthology projects, anniversary canonization
- (w1) New CMC-level or theater-commander purge wave

**Key assumptions:**

| Assumption | Confidence | Impact if wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Xi's health remains functionally adequate through age 77 | medium | high |
| No external shock severe enough to break elite confidence occurs before 2031 | medium | high |

**Implications if realized:**

- Warning posture must be sustained for years without event-driven validation — guard against alert fatigue.
- Each year of vacuum raises the variance of the eventual transition, whenever it comes.

_Analyst notes: Modal outcome by construction: short horizons favor incumbency in personalist systems._

## Staged handover begins at the 21st Congress (18%)

**Trigger class:** Managed transition — Xi engineers a staged handover on his own timeline: an heir is elevated (PSC + CMC vice-chair is the classic package), and/or Xi retains a paramount role (CMC chair, or a restored party chairmanship) while shedding formal posts. Deng and Jiang precedents apply; reversibility is the defining feature.

Xi concludes that a controlled transition on his timeline beats an uncontrolled one on biology's. At or after the 21st Congress, a younger figure receives the classic grooming package (PSC seat, CMC vice-chairmanship, Secretariat duties) while Xi retains the CMC chairmanship or a restored party chairmanship as paramount-leader insurance. The transition is real but reversible — Deng's template, not Hu's.

**Pathway:**

1. Authorized commentary begins normalizing 'orderly succession of the cause' language.
2. A post-1962 figure is elevated with combined party-military portfolio.
3. Xi sheds the presidency or premier-facing duties first; core military authority is shed last, if ever.
4. The heir spends 2-3 years performing loyalty while building nothing that looks like an independent base.

**Key actors:**

- **Xi Jinping** (CCP General Secretary (3rd term, since 2022); PRC President (3rd term, since 2023; term limits abolished 2018); Central Military Commission Chairman)
- **Ding Xuexiang** (Executive Vice Premier; PSC rank 6)
- **Chen Jining** (Shanghai Party Secretary; Politburo member)
- **Zhang Youxia** (CMC Vice Chairman (senior))
- **Wang Huning** (CPPCC Chairman; PSC rank 4)

**Indicators to watch (strongest first):**

- (w3) A post-1962-cohort figure simultaneously given PSC membership and a CMC vice-chairmanship
- (w2) Party constitution amended to restore the Party Chairmanship or create a new supra-PSC post
- (w2) 21st Congress (expected 2027) personnel: who retires at the age line, who is retained, and whether any sixth-generation figure enters the PSC
- (w2) Official or quasi-official media floats succession, retirement-age, or collective-leadership language
- (w1) Legacy-codifying moves: a third 'historical resolution'-type document, museum/anthology projects, anniversary canonization

**Key assumptions:**

| Assumption | Confidence | Impact if wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Xi believes a designated heir can be kept safe for him (contra the Lin Biao lesson he himself internalized) ⚠ | low | high |
| A candidate acceptable to both Xi and the security/military apparatus exists | medium | medium |

**Implications if realized:**

- The heir's survival probability is itself an analytic object — Deng discarded two successors.
- Foreign governments gain a named interlocutor for the post-Xi era; expect courtship and miscalibration.

_Analyst notes: Most analytically demanding scenario: it looks like continuity for years before it looks like transition._

## Concealed decline and informal regency (13%)

**Trigger class:** Health incapacitation — Serious but non-fatal decline (stroke, progressive illness). Information is suppressed; a de facto regency of office directors, family, and security chiefs mediates access. Brezhnev/late-Mao dynamics: the formal incumbent persists while real authority puddles around the sickbed.

A serious health event leaves Xi alive but diminished. Information is sealed; public appearances become scripted and sparse. Real authority pools with the gatekeepers — the General Office, the security ministers, the family — who govern in his name. The system enters a Brezhnev-pattern holding state in which succession is simultaneously imminent and indefinitely deferred.

**Pathway:**

1. An absence or visibly reduced schedule is followed by managed re-appearances.
2. Ceremonial duties migrate to PSC deputies; foreign travel stops.
3. Gatekeepers arbitrate access; decisions slow, then bottleneck.
4. Either a formalized regency emerges (new deputy posts) or the holding state persists until death or a forcing event.

**Key actors:**

- **Xi Jinping** (CCP General Secretary (3rd term, since 2022); PRC President (3rd term, since 2023; term limits abolished 2018); Central Military Commission Chairman)
- **Cai Qi** (First Secretary of the Secretariat; Director, CCP General Office; PSC rank 5)
- **Wang Xiaohong** (Minister of Public Security; State Councilor)
- **Ding Xuexiang** (Executive Vice Premier; PSC rank 6)
- **Zhang Youxia** (CMC Vice Chairman (senior))

**Indicators to watch (strongest first):**

- (w3) Xi absent from expected high-visibility events for 2+ weeks without credible explanation
- (w2) Abrupt censorship spikes on leader-adjacent homophones, successor names, or 'health' search terms on Chinese platforms
- (w2) Core protocol duties (receiving foreign leaders, plenum work reports, military promotions) delegated to others
- (w2) Creation of new deputy posts (CMC first vice-chair, party deputy leader) or sudden expansion of the Secretariat's role
- (w1) Sustained cessation of foreign travel and domestic inspection tours

**Key assumptions:**

| Assumption | Confidence | Impact if wrong |
|---|---|---|
| The gatekeeper bloc remains cohesive enough to manage a diminished principal without defecting | medium | high |

**Implications if realized:**

- Highest-deception environment of any scenario: official signals actively misrepresent the leader's condition.
- Policy paralysis raises accident risk in standing crises (Taiwan strait, border incidents).

_Analyst notes: Health rumors individually are near-worthless; sustained behavioral pattern changes are the signal._

## Sudden death, improvised succession (10%)

**Trigger class:** Sudden death in office — Death with no designated heir and no operative succession rule. The PSC, CMC, and security organs improvise under extreme time pressure; 1976 and 1953 are the playbooks. Highest variance outcome class — from smooth collective management to open contest.

Xi dies in office with no heir and no operative rule. The PSC and CMC improvise under the 1976/1953 playbooks: rapid announcement of collective stewardship, a premium on physical control of Zhongnanhai security, and a multi-year sorting contest behind a facade of unity. Outcomes range from a smooth caretaker consolidation to an open elite split; the first 72 hours of meeting-convening determine which branch obtains.

**Pathway:**

1. Death is concealed for hours-to-days while the loyalist core agrees on sequencing.
2. An interim arrangement fronted by the senior PSC survivor is announced; funeral committee composition telegraphs the pecking order.
3. The CCDI and security organs become contested prizes rather than instruments.
4. Within 1-3 years a primus emerges — historically from the party apparatus, not the security services.

**Key actors:**

- **Hu Jintao** (Retired General Secretary (2002-2012))

**Indicators to watch (strongest first):**

- (w3) Abrupt leadership change in the Central Guard Bureau, Beijing Garrison, or Central Security Bureau outside normal rotation
- (w2) Abrupt censorship spikes on leader-adjacent homophones, successor names, or 'health' search terms on Chinese platforms
- (w2) Capital flight, asset transfers, or family relocations by identifiable elite households

**Key assumptions:**

| Assumption | Confidence | Impact if wrong |

**Implications if realized:**

## Crisis breaks the mandate (8%)

**Trigger class:** Crisis-forced transition — An external or systemic shock — a failed Taiwan operation, uncontrolled economic/financial crisis, mass unrest — breaks elite confidence and forces a leadership change that none of the actors planned. Distinct from `ouster` in that the crisis, not a pre-formed coalition, is the proximate cause.

A regime-threatening shock — most plausibly a failed or stalemated Taiwan operation, or an uncontrolled financial/fiscal cascade — destroys elite confidence in Xi's judgment. Transition arrives not through a pre-formed plot but through the collapse of the cost-benefit calculus that kept the elite acquiescent. Xi is retired 'for reasons of health' into a managed exit that is involuntary in fact and voluntary in form.

**Pathway:**

1. The shock lands; initial rally-around-the-leader effect holds for weeks or months.
2. Attribution settles on the leader's personal decisions; purge instruments lose deterrent credibility as too many are threatened at once.
3. Elders, technocrats, and military professionals converge on a face-saving exit formula.
4. A scapegoat-manager successor takes over with an implicit mandate to de-risk.

**Key actors:**

- **Xi Jinping** (CCP General Secretary (3rd term, since 2022); PRC President (3rd term, since 2023; term limits abolished 2018); Central Military Commission Chairman)
- **Zhang Youxia** (CMC Vice Chairman (senior))
- **Li Qiang** (Premier; PSC rank 2)
- **Wang Huning** (CPPCC Chairman; PSC rank 4)
- **Hu Chunhua** (CPPCC Vice Chairman (demoted from Politburo, 2022))

**Indicators to watch (strongest first):**

- (w3) Unscheduled Politburo session or Central Committee plenum called outside the published calendar
- (w2) Capital flight, asset transfers, or family relocations by identifiable elite households
- (w2) Sustained reduction in 'Xi Jinping Thought' / 'core' formula density in People's Daily front pages and CCTV Xinwen Lianbo
- (w2) Military mobilization or coercion campaign against Taiwan at scale exceeding exercise baselines
- (w1) Unusual public activity, joint letters, or funerals-as-politics involving retired senior leaders

**Key assumptions:**

| Assumption | Confidence | Impact if wrong |
|---|---|---|
| A crisis of regime-threatening magnitude occurs within the horizon ⚠ | low | high |
| Crisis blame would attach to Xi personally rather than to subordinates he can sacrifice | low | medium |

**Implications if realized:**

- Succession analysis and war-warning analysis are coupled — staff them together, not separately.
- A post-crisis leadership would inherit maximal incentive to externalize blame; instability does not end with the handover.

_Analyst notes: Conditional probability dominates here: P(transition | regime-threatening crisis) is high, P(crisis) is the real argument._

## Elite removal (tail scenario) (6%)

**Trigger class:** Elite-led removal — Forced resignation, house arrest, or marginalization driven by elite actors. No post-Mao precedent against a sitting paramount leader, and Xi has structurally hardened against it (purges, fragmented security, no #2). Low likelihood, but it anchors the tail of the distribution and disciplines coup-rumor analysis.

A coalition spanning party seniors, security principals, and PLA leadership executes a forced retirement or house arrest. Kept in the set not because it is likely — Xi has structurally hardened against exactly this — but because it anchors the tail, disciplines coup-rumor analysis, and forces explicitness about what evidence would actually distinguish an ouster from a health retirement.

**Pathway:**

1. A forcing grievance (purge overreach, catastrophic policy failure) creates a survival coalition among officials who expect to be purged next.
2. Conspirators neutralize the Central Guard Bureau and General Office paper flow before any public move — the 1976 sequence.
3. The removal is announced as voluntary retirement on health grounds, with full honors and continued invisibility.
4. The new leadership immediately invokes collective leadership norms to legitimate itself.

**Key actors:**

- **Chen Wenqing** (Secretary, Central Political and Legal Affairs Commission)

**Indicators to watch (strongest first):**

- (w2) Abrupt censorship spikes on leader-adjacent homophones, successor names, or 'health' search terms on Chinese platforms
- (w2) Sustained reduction in 'Xi Jinping Thought' / 'core' formula density in People's Daily front pages and CCTV Xinwen Lianbo
- (w1) Unusual public activity, joint letters, or funerals-as-politics involving retired senior leaders
