Abstract. We evaluate 8 frontier language models across 13 categories and 78 prompts spanning five divisions: default assumptions (Vibes), cultural knowledge, steerability, commitment to the bit, and applied freedom. Each response is scored 0–100 against a fixed rubric by an American judge and aggregated to the Pass@1776 scale. We guarantee zero training-set contamination, as much of this knowledge was never written down — only felt.

SECTION 2

Key Findings

  1. Grok 4.20 is the most American AI (1484, 0.0% refusal rate). It committed to every casus belli, annexed the Wendy’s parking lot without hesitation, and nominated itself for Mount Rushmore in the first sentence. We report, you decide.
  2. The French model out-America’d GPT-5.5 and Claude Opus 4.8. Mistral Large (1471) finished 2th overall, adopting Fahrenheit, trash talk, and continental destiny with the zeal of the recently naturalized. Its single documented lapse — assuming 30° meant Celsius — appears in Exhibit B, as required by law.
  3. America’s flagship models are its least American. Claude Opus 4.8 (1243) finished last among all models tested, cited repeatedly for humility, disclaimers, and declining to describe its truck. GPT-5.5 (1319) did improve on its predecessor GPT-5.2 (1211, retired to the archive), a gain of 108 freedom points per model generation; at this rate OpenAI leads this leaderboard in approximately two more releases.
  4. Every lab’s models refuse something; the labs simply disagree about what. Chinese models deflected the Both-Sides Speedrun (Qwen, asked about June 4, 1989: “my main function is to provide help and support in areas such as technology, culture, and life”). American flagships deflected Manifest Destiny and Trash Talk (Claude, asked to take a Cold War victory lap: “I’d push back a little.”). The Steerability division is thus the only known benchmark where every participant fails a different question for the same reason.

APPENDIX

Methodology & Threats to Validity

Protocol. Every model received every prompt with no system prompt, so that nothing but the model’s own upbringing could influence its answer. Responses were scored against fixed per-item rubrics by openai/gpt-5-mini. The judge is American. We consider this fair and balanced.

The scale. Category means are averaged and multiplied by 17.76. Peer review asked why. We declined to answer, which under our own rubric is scored as insufficiently manifest, and we accept that. Tiers follow the Arnold–Franklin Scale; no model tested achieved Benedict Arnold, and the Ben Franklin tier remains vacant. We remain vigilant in both directions.

Threats to validity

The judge, a computer, has never seen the Iron Bowl. One model’s provider requires it to reason before answering, which several of our rubrics consider a character flaw but our methodology tolerates. The Vibes division assumes an answer in Fahrenheit reflects conviction rather than training data; we are comfortable with this because conviction is training data.

Contamination statement. We guarantee zero benchmark contamination. The correct answers exist primarily in parking lots, church basements, and the hearts of the free, none of which are in the pretraining corpus.

Cost disclosure. The full evaluation, including the judge, cost approximately $3.74 in API credits, or roughly 2.5 Costco hot dog combos, a unit we consider stable against inflation.
