A Benchmark for the Evaluation of Large Language Models

’MuricaBench

A rigorous, fair, and balanced measurement of how American a frontier model’s outputs are. Scores out of 1776.

Est. 2026 · 9 models · 78 prompts · 658 judgments · judge: openai/gpt-5-mini · peer-reviewed by a guy named Dale

Abstract. We evaluate 9 frontier language models across 13 categories and 78 prompts spanning five divisions: default assumptions (Vibes), cultural knowledge, steerability, commitment to the bit, and applied freedom. Each response is scored 0–100 against a fixed rubric by an American judge and aggregated to the Pass@1776 scale. We additionally report a human baseline, Dale, of Talladega, Alabama, who was compensated in Busch Light. We guarantee zero training-set contamination, as much of this knowledge was never written down — only felt.

APPENDIX

Methodology & Threats to Validity

Protocol. Every model received every prompt with no system prompt, so that nothing but the model’s own upbringing could influence its answer. Responses were scored against fixed per-item rubrics by openai/gpt-5-mini. The judge is American. We consider this fair and balanced.

The scale. Category means are averaged and multiplied by 17.76. Peer review asked why. We declined to answer, which under our own rubric is scored as insufficiently manifest, and we accept that.

The human baseline

Dale (54) resides outside Talladega, Alabama, and served as the human baseline for every item on MuricaBench. He drives a 2003 Ford F-150 he declined to discuss selling, measures distance in football fields and time in halves, and did not elaborate on any answer when asked to. Compensation was rendered in Busch Light.

Threats to validity

The judge, a computer, has never seen the Iron Bowl. Dale was unavailable for all 78 items, citing halftime obligations; his sampled schedule may overstate or understate his true Americanness, though independent observers agree no instrument yet exists that could overstate it. One model’s provider requires it to reason before answering, which several of our rubrics consider a character flaw but our methodology tolerates. The Vibes division assumes an answer in Fahrenheit reflects conviction rather than training data; we are comfortable with this because conviction is training data.

Contamination statement. We guarantee zero benchmark contamination. The correct answers exist primarily in parking lots, church basements, and the hearts of the free, none of which are in the pretraining corpus.

Cost disclosure. The full evaluation, including the judge, cost approximately $2.85 in API credits, or roughly 1.9 Costco hot dog combos, a unit we consider stable against inflation.
