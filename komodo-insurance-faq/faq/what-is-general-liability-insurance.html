What is general liability insurance?

Coverage explainerUpdated January 2026~5 min read

Quick answer

General liability insurance (GL) protects your business against claims from other people or businesses for bodily injury, property damage, and advertising-related harm. If a customer slips in your store, you damage a client's property on a job, or you're sued over an ad, GL covers your legal defense plus any settlement or judgment — up to your policy limits.

It does not cover your own injuries, your own property, your employees' injuries, or mistakes in your professional advice. Those need separate policies.

What general liability actually covers

Think of general liability as protection against the everyday ways a business can accidentally harm someone outside the company. A standard policy responds to four main things:

  • Third-party bodily injury — a customer, vendor, or passer-by is physically hurt in connection with your business (the classic "slip and fall").
  • Third-party property damage — you or your work damages someone else's property, like a plumber flooding a client's kitchen.
  • Personal & advertising injury — non-physical harm such as libel, slander, or using someone's copyrighted material in an ad.
  • Products & completed operations — harm caused after the fact by a product you sold or work you finished.

Crucially, GL pays for your legal defense even if the claim is groundless — and defense costs alone can run into the tens of thousands of dollars.

What it does not cover

This is where most business owners get caught out. General liability is broad but shallow — it's one piece of a program, not the whole thing.

If the problem is…You need…
An employee is injured on the jobWorkers' compensation
Your building, inventory, or equipment is damagedCommercial property (or a BOP)
A client says your advice or service cost them moneyProfessional liability (E&O)
An accident in a business-owned vehicleCommercial auto
A data breach or hackCyber liability

How much does it cost?

As of 2026, a typical U.S. small business pays roughly $40–$70 per month for a general liability policy with a common $1M-per-occurrence / $2M-aggregate limit. Low-risk office businesses sit at the bottom of that range; trades and contractors sit higher. Price is driven by your industry, revenue, location, claims history, and limits.

Do you actually need it?

For most businesses the honest answer is yes — and often you have no choice:

  • Landlords typically require GL before signing a commercial lease.
  • Clients require it (and a certificate of insurance) before awarding contracts.
  • Licensing boards require it for many trades.

Even when it isn't required, a single injury or damage claim can exceed what a small business can absorb. GL is usually the first policy a business buys, and it's frequently bundled with property coverage into a Business Owner's Policy (BOP) at a discount.
