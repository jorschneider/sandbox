General liability vs. professional liability

ComparisonUpdated January 2026~5 min read

Quick answer

General liability covers physical harm — someone is injured or their property is damaged (a client trips in your office). Professional liability (also called errors & omissions, or E&O) covers financial harm from your work — a client loses money because your advice or service was wrong, late, or negligent.

Neither covers the other's claims, so many service businesses carry both.

The simplest way to remember it

General liability is about what you break. Professional liability is about what you get wrong.

If a customer is physically hurt or their property is damaged, that's general liability. If a client says your work caused them a financial loss — bad advice, an error, a missed deadline — that's professional liability. A design firm that drops a laptop on a client's foot uses GL; the same firm that delivers plans with a costly error uses E&O.

Do you need both?

If your business both interacts with people/property and delivers professional work, then usually yes. Common examples of businesses that carry both:

  • Consultants and agencies
  • Accountants and bookkeepers
  • IT services and software firms
  • Architects, engineers, and designers
  • Real estate and insurance professionals
  • Healthcare and wellness providers

A pure e-commerce store with no advice component may only need GL; a remote-only advisor with no premises might lean mostly on E&O. Most fall in between.

Bonus: how does a BOP fit in?

People often confuse these with a Business Owner's Policy (BOP). A BOP isn't a competitor to GL — it's a bundle that includes GL:

PolicyWhat's inside
General liabilityThird-party injury & property damage only
Business Owner's Policy (BOP)General liability + commercial property + often business interruption, bundled at a discount

A BOP does not include professional liability or workers' comp — those are added on. If you want property protection too, a BOP is usually cheaper than buying GL and property separately.
