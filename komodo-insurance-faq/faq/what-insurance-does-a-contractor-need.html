What insurance does a contractor need?

Industry guideUpdated January 2026~6 min read

Quick answer

Most contractors need five things: general liability (third-party injury and property damage), workers' compensation once you have employees, commercial auto for work vehicles, tools & equipment coverage for gear on the job, and often a surety bond to meet licensing and client requirements.

Contractors who design or advise may also need professional liability, and bigger jobs often require an umbrella policy for higher limits.

Is contractor insurance required by law?

Partly. It helps to separate legally required from contractually required:

  • Workers' compensation is mandated in almost every U.S. state once you have employees. Texas is the main exception, and rules for sole proprietors and independent contractors vary by state.
  • Commercial auto liability is required for vehicles the business owns.
  • General liability is rarely required by statute — but most contractor licenses, general contractors, and project owners require proof of it (a certificate of insurance) before you can bid or start.
  • Surety bonds are frequently required to get or keep a contractor's license.
In practice, you'll usually be required to carry general liability and provide a certificate naming the client as an additional insured before you set foot on site.

How much does it cost?

As of 2026, general liability alone typically runs $90–$200+/month for a small contractor, and more for high-risk trades like roofing. A full program — GL, workers' comp, commercial auto, and tools — for a small crew often lands at $300–$900+/month, driven mostly by your trade, payroll, and revenue.
