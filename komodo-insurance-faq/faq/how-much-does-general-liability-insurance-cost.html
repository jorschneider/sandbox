How much does general liability insurance cost?

Cost guideUpdated January 2026~6 min read

Quick answer

As of 2026, most U.S. small businesses pay about $40–$70 per month (roughly $500–$850 a year) for general liability insurance with a standard $1M/$2M limit. Low-risk businesses like consultants pay under $40/month; higher-risk trades like contractors and roofers often pay $100+/month.

Your exact price depends on industry, revenue, location, claims history, and limits — and identical coverage can vary 30–50% between insurers, which is why shopping matters.

What drives your premium up or down

Underwriters price general liability on how likely you are to cause a claim and how expensive that claim could be. The biggest factors:

  • Industry & risk class — the single biggest factor. A bookkeeper and a demolition contractor are not in the same universe.
  • Annual revenue & payroll — more activity means more exposure, so premiums scale with size.
  • Location — state laws, litigation climate, and even crime rates move the price.
  • Claims history — past claims signal future ones and raise your rate.
  • Coverage limits & deductible — higher limits cost more; higher deductibles cost less.
  • Years in business — established, experienced operators often earn better rates.

Why did my premium go up?

Even with no claims, renewals can rise. Common reasons in 2026: general "social inflation" (rising jury awards and legal costs), your revenue or payroll grew, you added a higher-risk service, a claim was filed, or your insurer re-rated your whole class of business. If it jumped sharply, that's a signal to re-shop — not to just accept it.

7 ways to lower your general liability cost

  1. Compare multiple carriers. The same coverage routinely varies 30–50% between insurers. This is the biggest lever.
  2. Bundle into a BOP. Combining liability + property in a Business Owner's Policy is usually cheaper than buying them separately.
  3. Pay annually. Many insurers add a fee for monthly installments.
  4. Raise your deductible — if you can absorb the higher out-of-pocket on a claim.
  5. Get classified correctly. A wrong industry code can overcharge you for years.
  6. Improve safety & claims history. Documented safety practices reduce risk and rate.
  7. Right-size your limits. Enough to meet contracts and cover real exposure — not more.
