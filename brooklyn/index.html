How to read Brooklyn

Brooklyn was its own city until 1898 (America's third largest), stitched together from six original Dutch and English towns. That's why it has no single "center" — it has several Brooklyns, and the fastest way to understand it is to learn the big color zones on this map:

1. Downtown & Civic Brooklyn (blue) — the skyscraper/office/courthouse core, Brooklyn's version of Midtown.
2. North Brooklyn (pink) — Williamsburg, Greenpoint, Bushwick: former factory land that became the global symbol of "hipster Brooklyn," now increasingly luxury.
3. Brownstone Brooklyn (amber) — Park Slope, Carroll Gardens & co.: leafy 1880s rowhouse streets, strollers and restaurant rows.
4. Central Brooklyn (violet) — Bed-Stuy, Crown Heights, Flatbush's eastern half: the historic heart of Black and Caribbean Brooklyn.
5. The rest is its own world — Hasidic Borough Park, the Chinatowns of Sunset Park and Bensonhurst, Russian Brighton Beach, and a genuinely suburban southeast with boats in driveways.

It's 3D: drag with the right mouse button (or two fingers on a trackpad/phone) to tilt and spin. The terrain is exaggerated ~2.5× so you can actually see Brooklyn's geography: the glacial ridge that runs through Green-Wood Cemetery, Sunset Park and Highland Park is the borough's spine — everything south of it slopes to the sea. Downtown's towers pop up when you zoom in close. The ⛰ button turns terrain off.

Downtown Brooklyn vs. Williamsburg

The two are 3 miles apart and totally different animals:

DowntownWilliamsburg
What it isThe borough's central business district: courts, Borough Hall, offices, college campuses, mallsA former industrial waterfront district that became the world's shorthand for "cool Brooklyn"
Looks likeGlass towers (incl. Brooklyn's tallest, the 93-story Brooklyn Tower), wide avenues, chain storesLow warehouses, lofts, street art, plus a wall of new glass condos along the East River
Daytime crowdOffice workers, jurors, students, shoppersCreatives, tourists, brunch lines, stroller-pushing transplants
NightlifeQuiet — people commute outOne of the best bar/music/restaurant scenes in NYC
TrainsNearly every line: 2 3 4 5 A C F G B D N Q RBasically the L (plus G, J M Z on the edges) and the ferry
