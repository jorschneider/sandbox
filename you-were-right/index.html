PART III Matters Not Subject to Rescission

0

EUV lithography systems ever shipped to China. ASML says it can account for every EUV system it has ever built — all 314 in operation worldwide, plus the 26 since decommissioned — and not one is in China. The most consequential number in this field has not moved, and it is not moved by notices.

Consider what the zero is protecting. To make extreme-ultraviolet light, a machine built by one company in Veldhoven fires 50,000 droplets of molten tin per second into a vacuum and hits each one with a laser twice — pre-pulse and main pulse — because even photons believe in checking end-use twice. The resulting plasma burns at roughly 220,000 °C, about forty times the surface of the sun. The light bounces off Zeiss mirrors so flat that, by Zeiss’s own analogy, scaled to the size of Germany their tallest bump would stand about a tenth of a millimeter — their Zugspitze, as Zeiss puts it. The light source is developed and built by Cymer, of San Diego, California: the most advanced machine humanity makes cannot switch on without American hardware. It ships to a customer in roughly forty freight containers, on twenty trucks, aboard three Boeing 747s.

Nobody has ever smuggled an EUV machine — whatever this summer’s rumor says, ASML can point to every unit on its telemetry. It arrives in three 747s accompanied by 250 Dutch engineers, all of whom have LinkedIn.

That is not a policy that can be rescinded. That is a quarter century, tens of billions of dollars, and a supplier tree thousands of firms deep, which no notice in any register can conjure for anyone else.

PART IV Asymmetry Analysis: Rules Are Reversible. Fabs Are Not.

§ 4.1 The restore path

The distance between today and restored controls is one interim final rule, effective upon publication — as October 7, 2022 demonstrated to everyone who said it couldn’t be done. And the fastest tool in the drawer is faster still: on August 26, 2022, a single “is informed” letter imposed a license requirement on A100 and H100 exports to China the day it arrived. No rulemaking. No comment period. One letter.

§ 4.2 The rebuild path

The distance between China and an indigenous EUV ecosystem is measured in decades, defect densities, and the thousands of suppliers behind a single scanner. Its most advanced homegrown EUV effort produces 13.5nm light but cannot yet pattern a working chip; analysts put domestic sub-10nm-capable lithography no earlier than about 2030. Meanwhile teardowns keep finding 2020-vintage TSMC dies inside the current flagship Ascend — a stockpile is a wasting asset. And TSMC’s $165 billion pour of Arizona concrete cannot be un-built by anyone’s notice.

A loosening burns calendar time; it does not mint the other side a Zeiss, a Cymer, or twenty years of learning curves. The asymmetry runs in your favor, and it runs every single day, including the bad ones.

PART V Precedent: The Pendulum Has a Return Stroke

  1. 1987

    Toshiba–Kongsberg. Years of ignored diversion warnings, then quieter Soviet submarine propellers — then Japan rewrote its export-control law within about a year, executives were convicted, and allied enforcement came back stronger than it had ever been.

  2. 1994–96

    COCOM. The regime did not merely weaken; it was dissolved outright in 1994. By 1996 the allies had rebuilt it as the Wassenaar Arrangement, forty-two states strong today. This field has literally died and been resurrected. A loosening does not frighten it.

  3. 1996–99

    Commercial satellites. Loosened to Commerce in 1996; hauled back onto the Munitions List by 1999 once the evidence landed. The pendulum keeps its own docket.

  4. 2018

    ECRA. Congress codified into statute what a small band of people had spent years saying to half-empty conference rooms.

  5. 2022

    October 7. Written by people who spent years losing the interagency argument, right up until the day they won it.

§ 5.2 Matters presently in motion

The Chip Security Act — location verification on exported advanced AI chips, plus mandatory diversion reporting — cleared the House Foreign Affairs Committee 42–0 in March 2026. The GAIN AI Act — American buyers get first claim on advanced AI chips — was carried through the Senate in the October 2025 NDAA on a sponsor bench running from Banks and Cotton to Warren and Schumer; stripped in conference, it survives as standalone bipartisan bills in both chambers. Appropriators’ FY2026 deal funds BIS at $235 million — a 23 percent increase — tied specifically to semiconductor and AI export enforcement. The bills are not law yet, and you would be the first to say so; the BIS money already is, signed in January. All of it is the pendulum, loading.
