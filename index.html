About these lists & how they were sourced

The events were real: on July 4, 2026, New York Harbor hosted both the Sail4th 250 / America250 International Aerial Review and the International Parade of Sail — described as the largest maritime gathering in American history. Use the Sky / Sea toggle above to switch between the two field guides.

News outlets confirmed the totals, the Blue Angels leading the flyover, the allied aerobatic teams, and the tall-ship fleet with the USCGC Eagle at its head — but no agency published a full airframe-by-airframe manifest of the 200+ aircraft. So the demonstration teams and the ships shown here are individually confirmed; the U.S. military aircraft are the types documented as making up the America250 formation. The running orders are curated sequences (the ships follow the Parade-of-Sail lineup; the aircraft are grouped as a spectator would see the waves build), not official minute-by-minute timetables.

Every card offers two views. The drawings are recognition-style silhouettes, like the ones used in aircraft-identification training. The photographs are free-licensed images (public domain, Creative Commons, and similar) sourced from Wikimedia and stored with this page. Cards wearing the gold ★ at the parade badge — 16 of them, plus the whole gallery below — show photographs taken at the July 4, 2026 event itself, by military photographers and Commons contributors who were on the water and the waterfront — these are what cards open on by default. Cards without a parade photo open on their drawing; flip them to see a photo of the same aircraft type or the same named ship on another day, since news photography from the parade is copyrighted. Unit prices on the aircraft cards are approximate, publicly cited figures — flyaway or contract unit costs from different budget years, so treat them as ballpark, not invoices.
