    ⚠️ Please do not take this to your doctor

    This is a joke with footnotes. Observational studies report associations — people who do X tend to live longer — not years you can withdraw from an ATM. The effect sizes here are drawn from real, frequently-cited papers, then treated with the reckless literalism of a health-influencer thread.

    That's why there are two numbers. The big glowing one adds every association together as if they never overlap. The "with a straight face" number applies the thing real epidemiologists do — diminishing returns — because your third healthy habit does not pay off like your first, and you cannot become immortal by stacking press releases. The gap between the two numbers is the joke.

    In reality the effects confound, cancel, and mostly reflect the sort of person who already flosses. Optimists don't live longer because they're optimistic; people who can afford saunas can afford a lot of things. A daily glass of wine "adding" years is the most famous correlation ever to be quietly walked back. You cannot out-habit your genes, your zip code, or a bus.
